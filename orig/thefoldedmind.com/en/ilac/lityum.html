Lithium

Lithium salt

Used in bipolar disorder; found superior to placebo for mania at ages 7–17. Its narrow safety margin calls for blood level monitoring.

Site of action

Target: Alters sodium transport in nerve and muscle cells and affects serotonin and noradrenaline reuptake; its mechanism in mood is not known for certain (KÜB). B

No pathway or regional highlight is drawn because the mechanism is not known for certain. In ENIGMA, lithium users had a larger thalamus; this is an exploratory association and does not show causation (Hibar et al., 2016). B

The anatomy of side effects

Narrow safety margin

Blood level, kidney, thyroid; no brain pathway drawn

Toxicity is closely tied to blood level and can occur at treatment doses. Blood level, kidney, thyroid and cardiac function are monitored regularly; it is contraindicated in severe renal failure (KÜB). A

Pregnancy

No brain pathway drawn

It crosses the placenta; cardiac anomalies, especially Ebstein anomaly, are reported. Effective contraception is advised for women able to have children (KÜB). A

Children and adolescents

  • Turkey (product information, KÜB, checked in November 2026): should not be used under age 12 because efficacy and safety are unknown.
  • FDA 2018: acute mania and maintenance at ages 7–17.
  • In an 8-week placebo-controlled trial of 81 children aged 7–17, mania scores fell more (Findling et al., 2015). A

Interactions

  • NSAIDs, ACE inhibitors, angiotensin receptor antagonists, diuretics and some antibiotics (metronidazole, tetracyclines, co-trimoxazole, trimethoprim) can raise lithium levels (KÜB). A
  • Serotonin syndrome can occur with SSRIs (KÜB). A
  • Neurotoxicity can develop with CNS-active drugs even at normal blood levels; the KÜB advises avoiding use with antipsychotics. A
