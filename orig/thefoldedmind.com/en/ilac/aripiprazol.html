Aripiprazole

D2 partial agonist

Occupies nearly all D2 receptors without fully blocking them: usually does not raise prolactin, among the lower metabolic burdens in its class; keep akathisia in mind.

Site of action

Target: D2 and 5-HT1A partial agonist, 5-HT2A antagonist; D2 occupancy 87% in the putamen, 93% in the caudate, 91% in the ventral striatum (Mamo et al., 2007). B

A partial agonist occupies the receptor but only half-activates it: it turns the signal down when dopamine is high and does not cut it off when dopamine is low. So even at very high occupancy not everyone has extrapyramidal effects: in a 12-patient PET study they were seen in 2 of 4 patients with occupancy above 90% (Mamo et al., 2007). In the cortex, 5-HT2A is blocked. B

Blue pulse in the atlas: where cortical 5-HT2A receptors are blocked (in the striatum the signal is turned down, not cut)

The anatomy of side effects

Movement side effects

Nigrostriatal pathway: SNc → putamen and caudate

Akathisia and other extrapyramidal complaints can occur; in a small PET study they were seen in 2 of 4 patients with D2 occupancy above 90% (Mamo et al., 2007). B

Prolactin

Tuberoinfundibular pathway: hypothalamus → pituitary

Prolactin does not rise and may fall: −2.8 ng/mL vs placebo in children (Rogdaki et al., 2024). Partial agonism at the pituitary. A

Hypothalamus & Pituitary

Sleepiness, appetite and weight

Histamine (H1) and 5-HT2C: hypothalamus

Weight and metabolic burden among the lowest of the second-generation antipsychotics compared: +0.9 kg in short-term trials (Cohen et al., 2012), +4.4 kg in ~11 weeks with no significant metabolic change (Correll et al., 2009). A

Children and adolescents

  • FDA: schizophrenia 13–17, bipolar I 10–17, irritability in autism 6–17, Tourette’s 6–18.
  • EMA: schizophrenia age 15 and over; bipolar I mania age 13 and over for up to 12 weeks. Turkey: the product information (KÜB) of the generic product checked lists only schizophrenia in adults and adolescents (13–17); not recommended under 13; may differ by product.
  • Tourette’s (133 patients): difference in tic score −9.9 at the higher dose and −6.3 at the lower dose (Sallee et al., 2017). B

Interactions

  • Broken down by CYP2D6 and CYP3A4: halve the dose with a strong 2D6 (fluoxetine, paroxetine) or 3A4 inhibitor and in CYP2D6 poor metabolizers; increase it with 3A4 inducers (FDA label). A
