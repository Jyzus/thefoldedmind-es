Risperidone

Second-generation antipsychotic

The first large multicenter trial for irritability in autism (RUPP, 2002) used this drug; strong D2 and very strong 5-HT2A blockade; among the clearest prolactin rises in pediatric studies.

Site of action

Target: D2 and 5-HT2A antagonist (also α1, α2, H1); D2 occupancy 63–89% with continued use (Kapur et al., 1999). B

With the D2 receptor blocked, the dopamine signal is cut in the striatum; in the cortex, 5-HT2A blockade is even stronger. The clinical response becomes clear once striatal D2 occupancy exceeds 65% (PET study with haloperidol; Kapur et al., 2000). B

The anatomy of side effects

Movement side effects

Nigrostriatal pathway: SNc → putamen and caudate

Above 78% D2 occupancy, the likelihood of parkinsonism, dystonia and akathisia rises markedly (PET study with haloperidol; Kapur et al., 2000). B

Prolactin

Tuberoinfundibular pathway: hypothalamus → pituitary

With pituitary D2 receptors blocked, prolactin is released from its brake. In pediatric studies prolactin was elevated in 82–87% vs 3–7% on placebo (FDA label); in a network meta-analysis in children and adolescents, the largest rise among the drugs compared (+26.4 ng/mL; Rogdaki et al., 2024). A

Hypothalamus & Pituitary

Sleepiness, appetite and weight

Histamine (H1) and 5-HT2C: hypothalamus

+5.3 kg in ~11 weeks (Correll et al., 2009); sleepiness, increased appetite. B

Children and adolescents

  • FDA: irritability in autism ages 5–16, schizophrenia 13–17, bipolar I mania 10–17.
  • EMA: short-term treatment of persistent aggression in conduct disorder in children over 5 with intellectual disability.
  • Turkey: the originator’s product information (KÜB, 2011) lists, in children and adolescents, conduct and other disruptive behavior disorders in which aggressive or disruptive behavior is prominent (limited experience under 5), irritability associated with autistic disorder (aggression, self-injury, temper tantrums), bipolar mania (limited experience under 10) and schizophrenia in adolescents (limited experience under 13); generic products may differ.
  • RUPP (autism, 101 children): response 69% vs 12%; weight +2.7 vs +0.8 kg. A

Interactions

  • Broken down by CYP2D6: fluoxetine and paroxetine raise its levels, carbamazepine lowers them; the label calls for dose adjustment. A
  • Together with methylphenidate, a dose change of either drug may raise the risk of extrapyramidal symptoms (FDA label). A
  • α1 blockade adds to the orthostatic effect of other blood-pressure-lowering drugs (e.g. guanfacine).
