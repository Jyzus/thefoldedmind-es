Methylphenidate

Stimulant

Blocks the dopamine and noradrenaline transporters; the preferred first-choice medication for children and adolescents with ADHD in a network meta-analysis (2018). Its effect starts within hours.

Open in the 3D atlas → All medications

Site of action

Target: DAT and NET blockade; at therapeutic doses more than half of DAT is occupied (Volkow et al., 1998), and NET is also clearly occupied (Hannestad et al., 2010). B

With the transporters blocked, dopamine rises in the striatum and prefrontal cortex, and noradrenaline in the prefrontal cortex (Volkow et al., 2001). Stimulants increase activity in the right inferior frontal cortex and insula (Rubia et al., 2014) and bring the suppression of the default mode network during tasks toward normal (Peterson et al., 2009). B

Orange pulse in the atlas: where dopamine and noradrenaline rise (striatum and prefrontal cortex)

Children and adolescents

  • FDA and EMA: ADHD age 6 and over; also age 6 and over in the Turkish product information (KÜB) checked in October 2026.
  • The preferred first choice for children and adolescents in a network meta-analysis; SMD −0.78 on clinician ratings (Cortese et al., 2018). A
  • In randomized trials, the risk of new or worsening tics with stimulants did not differ from placebo (risk ratio 0.99; Cohen et al., 2015). A
  • Long-term stimulant use was associated with ~2.5 cm lower adult height (MTA follow-up, observational; Swanson et al., 2017). B

Interactions

  • Contraindicated with an MAOI or within 14 days of stopping one (hypertensive crisis). A
  • Together with risperidone, a risk of extrapyramidal symptoms when doses change (FDA label). A
  • SSRIs: no separate item in the label’s interactions section, but postmarketing reports list serotonin syndrome in combination with serotonergic drugs (FDA label). Broken down by carboxylesterase, not by CYP enzymes.
