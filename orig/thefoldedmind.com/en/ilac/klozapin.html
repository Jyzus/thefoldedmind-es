Clozapine

Second-generation antipsychotic

Used in treatment-resistant schizophrenia; low D2 occupancy and high 5-HT2 occupancy. The risks of agranulocytosis and myocarditis call for close monitoring.

Site of action

Target: On PET, D2 occupancy 20–67%, 5-HT2 occupancy 84–94%, D1 occupancy 36–59%; this combination differs from classical antipsychotics (Nordström et al., 1995). B

Weakly blocks D2 (striatum) and strongly blocks 5-HT2 (cortex) receptors; its anticholinergic, antihistaminic and anti-alpha-adrenergic effects are also strong (Turkish product information, KÜB). B

The anatomy of side effects

Agranulocytosis

Blood count monitoring; no brain pathway drawn

It can cause agranulocytosis; frequency and fatality fall with white cell and absolute neutrophil monitoring (KÜB). A

Myocarditis and cardiomyopathy

No brain pathway drawn

Myocarditis, sometimes fatal, has been reported; risk is highest in the first 2 months. If suspected, the drug is stopped without delay (KÜB). A

Sleepiness, appetite and weight

Histamine (H1) and 5-HT2C: hypothalamus

Sedation, weight gain, hyperglycemia and dyslipidemia are reported; severe hyperglycemia progressing to ketoacidosis occurs rarely (KÜB). A

Anticholinergic effects

Muscarinic receptors: cortex and thalamus

SPECT has shown that it blocks muscarinic receptors in the brain: receptor availability fell in the basal ganglia, cortex and pons, and most in the thalamus (8 patients; Raedler et al., 2003). Bowel problems ranging from constipation to obstruction and paralytic ileus come from receptors in the body, probably through its anticholinergic properties (KÜB). B

Children and adolescents

  • Turkey (product information, KÜB, checked in November 2026): should not be used under age 16 because safety and efficacy are not established.
  • In refractory early-onset schizophrenia, a double-blind trial of 39 adolescents found response at 12 weeks of 66%, versus 33% with high-dose olanzapine; the sample is small and the finding preliminary (Kumra et al., 2008). B

Interactions

  • Should not be combined with drugs known to cause agranulocytosis (KÜB). A
  • With benzodiazepines or other psychotropics, especially during rapid titration, collapse and respiratory arrest have been reported (KÜB). A
  • CYP1A2 inhibitors raise levels: caution with fluvoxamine; caffeine also raises plasma levels (KÜB). A
