Psychopathology · diagnosis

Borderline personality disorder

A personality disorder with persistent instability of emotions, self-image and relationships, fear of abandonment, impulsivity, self-harm and a chronic feeling of emptiness. The emotion regulation circuit stands out in the brain: in response to negative emotional stimuli, the amygdala overreacts and the dorsolateral PFC underreacts.

Clinical picture (children and adolescents)

  • The diagnosis can be made in adolescence; symptoms emerge during this period and early intervention is recommended. Prevalence in adults is about 1–3%. B
  • Subthreshold borderline symptoms in adolescence predict difficulties in functioning over the following 20 years (Winograd et al., 2008; community sample). C
  • Self-harm and suicidal behavior are major risks. A

Circuits and networks

  • Amygdala: overreacts to negative emotional stimuli; gray matter volume smaller (Schulze et al., 2016; 19 functional studies, 281 patients). B
  • Dorsolateral Prefrontal Cortex: blunted response during negative emotions (Schulze et al., 2016). B
  • Anterior Cingulate Cortex (especially subgenual) and Insula: emotional pain and bodily sensation. B
  • Posterior Cingulate Cortex: increased activity (Schulze et al., 2016). B
  • Orbitofrontal Cortex and Medial & Frontopolar Prefrontal Cortex: impulse control and emotion regulation. B
  • Temporoparietal Junction and Medial & Frontopolar Prefrontal Cortex: mentalizing (understanding others' and one's own mind); the target of mentalization-based treatment. C

Neurotransmitter systems

  • A link between serotonin and impulsivity has been reported; there is no approved medication for borderline disorder. B
  • The opioid system and the social pain hypothesis. C

Imaging and neurobiological findings

  • Multimodal meta-analysis: the left amygdala is both functionally overactive and smaller in gray matter volume; amygdala hyperactivity is clear in unmedicated samples (Schulze et al., 2016). Findings are inconsistent across studies. B

Developmental course

  • Early childhood trauma and difficulties in the relationship with caregivers are risk factors; symptoms may decrease over time. B

Treatment and the systems it targets

  • Psychotherapy is the main treatment: dialectical behavior therapy (DBT) is the most studied, followed by mentalization-based treatment (MBT). A
  • DBT for adolescents (DBT-A): in 12–18-year-olds with repeated self-harm, a clear reduction in suicidal ideation and depression compared with enhanced usual care; the advantage in self-harm frequency lasted through 3-year follow-up (Mehlum et al., 2014; 2019). B
  • Medication is not recommended for borderline disorder itself; it is used for co-occurring conditions. B
  • The place of medication: Cochrane 2022: no medication seems effective in specifically treating borderline pathology, and adverse event reporting is poor. Medication decisions for co-occurring conditions are assessed separately by the physician. A
  • Schema therapy: In a 3-year randomized trial of 88 patients, both treatments improved symptoms and quality of life; schema therapy outperformed transference-focused psychotherapy with less dropout. The trial is in adults. B
