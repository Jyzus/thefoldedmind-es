Psychopathology · diagnosis

ADHD

A neurodevelopmental disorder beginning in childhood, in which sustained attention, impulse control and activity level differ markedly from what is expected for age. The most consistent brain findings are in fronto-striatal circuits, the dopamine and noradrenaline systems and the balance of attention networks; imaging differences are small and not diagnostic.

Clinical picture (children and adolescents)

  • Core domains: inattention (difficulty sustaining attention, forgetfulness, disorganization), hyperactivity and impulsivity (restlessness, difficulty waiting one's turn, interrupting). Symptoms begin before age 12, appear in more than one setting (home, school) and impair functioning. A
  • Prevalence: about 3.4% of children and adolescents worldwide (Polanczyk et al., 2015); 5.3% in an earlier meta-regression (Polanczyk et al., 2007). The difference between estimates comes largely from diagnostic criteria and methods, not from country. A
  • Sex: diagnosed more often in boys; the gap is larger in clinical than in community samples. In girls, a quieter, mainly inattentive presentation can delay diagnosis (Faraone et al., 2021). B
  • Across development: hyperactivity decreases with age, while inattention and inner restlessness persist longer. In a substantial share of those diagnosed in childhood, symptoms continue to affect functioning in adulthood (Faraone et al., 2021). A
  • Common co-occurring conditions: oppositional defiant disorder, specific learning disorder, anxiety disorders, tic disorders, autism spectrum disorder, developmental coordination disorder; in adolescence, substance use and accidents. A

Circuits and networks

  • Dorsolateral Prefrontal Cortex (dorsolateral prefrontal cortex): working memory, planning and top-down attentional control; the delay in cortical maturation is most marked in frontal regions (Shaw et al., 2007). B
  • Anterior Cingulate Cortex (anterior cingulate cortex): error monitoring and conflict detection; its connectivity with the DMN is altered during attentional fluctuations (Castellanos et al., 2008). B
  • Caudate Nucleus, Putamen (striatum) and Nucleus Accumbens (nucleus accumbens): fronto-striatal loops and reward/motivation. In ENIGMA, accumbens (d = −0.15), caudate (d = −0.11) and putamen (d = −0.14) volumes were smaller; differences were clearer in children (Hoogman et al., 2017). A
  • Amygdala and Hippocampus: in ENIGMA, amygdala (d = −0.19) and hippocampus (d = −0.11) volumes were smaller; linked to difficulties in emotion regulation (Hoogman et al., 2017). A
  • Ventral Tegmental Area and Substantia Nigra (midbrain dopamine nuclei): sources of mesolimbic and nigrostriatal dopamine; the system targeted by medication.
  • Locus Coeruleus (locus coeruleus): source of noradrenaline; tunes arousal and attention.
  • Cerebellum: timing and motor control; fronto-cerebellar loops.
  • Inferior Parietal Lobule and Superior Parietal Lobule (parietal cortex): posterior nodes of the attention networks.
  • Networks: the Frontoparietal control network (frontoparietal control) and Dorsal attention network (dorsal attention) networks do not engage enough during tasks; the Default mode network (DMN) (default mode) is not silenced enough during tasks and causes lapses of attention (the "default mode interference" hypothesis; Sonuga-Barke and Castellanos, 2007). This is at the level of a hypothesis. C

Neurotransmitter systems

  • Dopamine (dopamine): reward processing and motivation; in unmedicated adults, dopamine transporter and D2/D3 receptor binding in the reward pathway were low and related to inattention (Volkow et al., 2009). The association is moderate to weak. B
  • Noradrenaline (noradrenaline): α2A receptors in the prefrontal cortex are needed for working memory and attention; atomoxetine and guanfacine target this system. B
  • Heritability is high: 74% on average in twin studies (Faraone and Larsson, 2019). Individual genetic variants have very small effects; risk comes from the sum of many genes. A

Imaging and neurobiological findings

  • ENIGMA-ADHD (1,713 with ADHD, 1,529 controls): accumbens, amygdala, caudate, hippocampus, putamen and intracranial volume smaller; effect sizes small (d = −0.10 to −0.19), largest in the amygdala. Differences were clear under age 15 and not significant in adults. Medication use and comorbid diagnoses did not change the results (Hoogman et al., 2017). A
  • These are group-level differences; brain imaging cannot diagnose ADHD in an individual. A

Developmental course

  • The cortex matures in the normal order (back to front) but is delayed by about 3 years on average; the delay is greatest in frontal regions. The motor cortex matures slightly earlier (Shaw et al., 2007; 446 children, 824 MRI scans). B
  • This finding supports seeing ADHD as a maturational delay rather than a permanent "impairment"; however, the delay cannot be detected in a single person's scan. B

Treatment and the systems it targets

  • Preschool (under 5): parent training programs are first line; medication is considered only with a second specialist opinion (NICE NG87). A
  • Behavioral interventions (parent training, classroom adjustments): small to moderate effects on symptoms and co-occurring oppositional problems; the effect is largest for raters closest to treatment; 21 studies, more than 2,200 children (Groenman et al., 2022). B
  • Medication (children and adolescents): methylphenidate is first choice in the short term; amphetamines in adults. Network meta-analysis of 133 double-blind trials (Cortese et al., 2018). Long-term data are limited. A
    • Methylphenidate: blocks the dopamine and noradrenaline transporters (DAT, NET); raises dopamine and noradrenaline in the striatum and prefrontal cortex. A
    • Amphetamines: besides blocking transporters, also increase dopamine and noradrenaline release. A
    • Atomoxetine: selective noradrenaline reuptake inhibitor; also raises dopamine in the prefrontal cortex. B
    • Guanfacine and clonidine: α2A adrenergic receptor agonists; strengthen signal transmission in the prefrontal cortex. B
  • Amphetamines: In a network meta-analysis of 133 double-blind trials, this group is the suggested first choice in adults, while methylphenidate is suggested in children and adolescents. Besides blocking transporters, it increases dopamine and noradrenaline release. Not used in Turkey; approved in the US. A
