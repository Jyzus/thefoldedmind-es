Thalamus

Thalamus

The gateway to the cortex: filters and relays every sense except smell; regulates wakefulness, attention and the motor loops.

Anatomy

  • The largest part of the diencephalon; two egg-shaped masses forming the lateral walls of the third ventricle, joined by the interthalamic adhesion.
  • Anterior, medial and lateral groups divided by the internal medullary lamina.
  • Nuclei: VPL/VPM (somatosensory), LGN (vision), MGN (hearing), VA/VL (motor), mediodorsal (PFC), anterior (limbic), pulvinar (visual attention), reticular (GABAergic gate), intralaminar (arousal).
  • Blood supply: PCA (thalamoperforating and thalamogeniculate arteries), posterior communicating and anterior choroidal arteries.

Substructures

7 substructures; each has its own page.

  • Anterior Nuclear Group ANThe thalamic station of the Papez circuit: relays information from the mammillary body to the cingulate cortex, supporting episodic memory and…
  • Mediodorsal Nucleus MDThe thalamic partner of the prefrontal cortex: carries out working memory, decision-making and reward learning together with prefrontal circuits.
  • Ventral Anterior Nucleus VAA motor planning station that relays basal ganglia output to premotor and prefrontal cortex.
  • Ventral Lateral Group (Dorsal Part) VLdThe dorsal part of the ventral lateral region: the area holding the relay nuclei that pass cerebellar and basal ganglia input to motor cortex.
  • Ventral Lateral Group (Ventral Part) VLvThe ventral part of the ventral lateral region: an area next to the motor and sensory relay nuclei, reciprocally connected with the cortex.
  • Central Lateral – Lateral Posterior – Medial Pulvinar CL-LP-MPuA group containing the arousal-related intralaminar central lateral nucleus and the lateral posterior and medial pulvinar nuclei linked to…
  • Pulvinar PulThe largest thalamic nucleus: regulates visual attention, salience and communication between cortical areas.

Functions

  • Relay and filtering of sensory information to the cortex (except smell).
  • Sleep–wake and level of consciousness; thalamocortical rhythms and sleep spindles (reticular nucleus).
  • Attention: the pulvinar and the reticular “gate” mechanism.
  • Relaying basal ganglia and cerebellar output to motor cortex (VA/VL).
  • Working memory and executive function (mediodorsal–PFC); memory (anterior nucleus — Papez).

Connections and chemistry

  • The whole cortex (thalamocortical and corticothalamic projections)
  • Basal ganglia (GPi → VA/VL)
  • Cerebellum (dentate → VL)
  • Hippocampus and mammillary body (anterior nucleus)
  • Glutamate (projection), GABA (reticular), acetylcholine and noradrenaline inputs

Development

Developmental course through childhood and adolescence.

  • Thalamocortical axons guide the mapping of cortical areas in the fetal period.
  • Sleep spindles appear in infancy (~2–3 months); thalamocortical connections mature through childhood.
  • Reduced thalamic volume and connectivity after preterm birth.

Clinical

  • Childhood absence epilepsy: a disorder of thalamocortical rhythm; T-type calcium channels are the target of ethosuximide. Ethosuximide and valproate are more effective than lamotrigine; because valproate causes attention problems more often, ethosuximide is the first choice (Glauser et al., 2010). A
  • Schizophrenia: reduced thalamic volume and PFC–thalamus connectivity (also in early-onset cases). In adult studies, sleep spindles (12–16 Hz NREM oscillations generated by the thalamic reticular nucleus) are markedly reduced; the finding is stable across the night, not due to antipsychotics and related to cognitive performance (Ferrarelli et al., 2007, 2010). A candidate biomarker. B
  • Tourette syndrome and OCD: the thalamic link of the CSTC circuit. In ENIGMA-OCD, the thalamus is larger in unmedicated children (d = 0.38), whereas in adults the pallidum is larger and the hippocampus smaller. The biology of child and adult OCD may differ (Boedhoe et al., 2017). A
  • Differences in sensory gating in ASD and schizophrenia.

Treatment

This region from the perspective of medication, psychotherapy and neuromodulation. Letter badges are evidence levels.

  • Absence epilepsy: start with ethosuximide; freedom from treatment failure at 16 weeks was 53% with ethosuximide, 58% with valproate and 29% with lamotrigine; attention problems in 49% with valproate vs 33% with ethosuximide (Glauser et al., 2010; 453 children). Ethosuximide is not enough if generalized tonic-clonic seizures are also present. A
  • Deep brain stimulation in Tourette syndrome: in an international registry (185 patients, 31 centers) the most frequent target was the centromedian thalamus; at 12 months motor tics fell by about 38% and vocal tics by 43%; stimulation-related speech disturbance and paresthesia are common but reversible (Martinez-Ramirez et al., 2018). Only in refractory, severe cases. C
  • Deep brain stimulation and focused ultrasound of the thalamic VIM nucleus in essential tremor (adults). B

Assessment

Neuropsychological tasks and measures that probe this region's function.

  • EEG: sleep spindles, 3 Hz spike-and-wave in absence seizures
  • Sensory gating: P50 suppression, prepulse inhibition (PPI)
  • Attention tests

Lesions and damage

Clinical findings expected with damage or dysfunction.

  • Thalamic pain syndrome (Dejerine-Roussy), contralateral sensory loss.
  • Thalamic amnesia (anterior/mediodorsal); Wernicke encephalopathy (thiamine deficiency — reported in adolescents with anorexia or after bariatric surgery).
  • Bilateral paramedian infarction: hypersomnia, apathy, vertical gaze palsy.
