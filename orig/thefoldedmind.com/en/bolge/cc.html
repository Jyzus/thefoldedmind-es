Corpus Callosum

Corpus callosum

The largest white matter bridge joining the two hemispheres; shares sensory, motor and cognitive information between them.

Anatomy

  • The largest commissure, made of about 200 million axons.
  • Parts: rostrum, genu (frontal — forceps minor), body (motor/somatosensory), isthmus, splenium (occipitotemporal — forceps major).
  • Roof of the lateral ventricles; the cingulate gyrus lies above, the fornix and septum pellucidum below.
  • Blood supply: pericallosal artery (ACA); splenium PCA.

Functions

  • Interhemispheric transfer of information and coordination.
  • Bimanual motor coordination.
  • Giving right-hemisphere information access to the language system (naming).
  • Interhemispheric inhibition and support of lateralization; processing speed.

Connections and chemistry

  • Homotopic cortical areas (both hemispheres)
  • Forceps minor (frontal), forceps major (occipital)
  • Cingulum bundle (above)

Development

Developmental course through childhood and adolescence.

  • Myelinates rapidly after birth; maturation continues into adolescence and young adulthood.
  • The decrease in mirror movements is linked to callosal maturation.
  • Cross-sectional area increases in childhood and adolescence.

Clinical

  • Agenesis or hypoplasia of the corpus callosum: about 1.8 per 10,000 births in a California population registry (3.4 million live births); about 4 times more common in preterm infants (California population study, 2008). Isolated or syndromic; associated with social-cognitive difficulties and ASD. Developmental outcomes in isolated cases vary widely, and prenatal counseling should reflect this. A
  • ASD: reduced CC volume and FA (meta-analyses).
  • Early-life neglect: reduced CC area (Teicher et al., 2004).
  • CC anomalies are common in fetal alcohol spectrum disorder and prematurity.

Treatment

This region from the perspective of medication, psychotherapy and neuromodulation. Letter badges are evidence levels.

  • In agenesis: developmental follow-up and early intervention (speech, motor, social skills); assessment for epilepsy and associated anomalies, genetic testing. B
  • After early neglect (Teicher et al., 2004), early placement in a safe caregiving environment and attachment-focused interventions are the strongest supports for development. B

Assessment

Neuropsychological tasks and measures that probe this region's function.

  • Dichotic listening, cross-localization of fingers
  • Bimanual coordination tasks
  • Fractional anisotropy (FA) measures with DTI

Lesions and damage

Clinical findings expected with damage or dysfunction.

  • Disconnection syndrome (split brain): tactile anomia and apraxia of the left hand.
  • Alien hand syndrome; alexia without agraphia with a left occipital + splenium lesion.
