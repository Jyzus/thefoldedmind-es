Pons

Pons Varolii

The bridge between the cortex and the cerebellum; source of noradrenaline (locus coeruleus) and serotonin (raphe), and generator of REM sleep.

Anatomy

  • The middle part of the brainstem. Ventral basilar part: pontine nuclei and transverse fibers → middle cerebellar peduncle.
  • Dorsal tegmentum: locus coeruleus (noradrenaline), raphe nuclei (serotonin), pontine reticular formation, cholinergic PPN/LDT (REM).
  • Nuclei of cranial nerves V, VI, VII and VIII; medial longitudinal fasciculus (MLF).
  • Blood supply: basilar artery (paramedian and circumferential branches), AICA.

Functions

  • Corticopontocerebellar pathway: relays motor plans from the cortex to the cerebellum.
  • Locus coeruleus: arousal, attention (an inverted-U relationship with performance), stress response.
  • Raphe: serotonergic regulation of mood, impulsivity, sleep and appetite.
  • REM sleep and REM atonia; sleep–wake transitions.
  • Facial sensation and chewing (V), facial expression (VII), hearing and balance (VIII), lateral gaze (VI), adjustment of breathing rhythm.

Connections and chemistry

  • Locus coeruleus → whole cortex, hippocampus, amygdala, thalamus (noradrenaline)
  • Raphe → cortex, limbic system, striatum (serotonin)
  • Pontine nuclei → cerebellum
  • PPN/LDT → thalamus (acetylcholine)

Development

Developmental course through childhood and adolescence.

  • REM sleep makes up about half of total sleep in the newborn and decreases in childhood.
  • The ABR is an index of brainstem maturation in preterm infants.
  • Serotonin also acts as a neurotrophic signal in early brain development.

Clinical

  • Anxiety and panic: LC hyperactivity. PTSD: hyperarousal, nightmares (the α1 antagonist prazosin, α2 agonists).
  • ADHD: noradrenergic treatments (atomoxetine; the α2A agonists guanfacine and clonidine).
  • Depression, anxiety, OCD: serotonergic treatments (SSRIs).
  • Cataplexy in narcolepsy (REM atonia intruding into wakefulness); nightmare disorder.
  • Diffuse midline glioma (H3 K27M, formerly DIPG): a pontine (also thalamic and spinal) tumor mostly affecting children aged 3–10; standard treatment is radiotherapy, and prognosis is poor. A
  • Möbius syndrome (involvement of nerves VI and VII); co-occurrence with ASD has been reported.

Treatment

This region from the perspective of medication, psychotherapy and neuromodulation. Letter badges are evidence levels.

  • Dordaviprone (ONC201; a D2/3 antagonist and mitochondrial ClpP activator): FDA accelerated approval (August 2025) from age 1 for H3 K27M-mutant diffuse midline glioma that has progressed despite prior treatment; response rate 22% in 50 patients, median duration of response 10.3 months. The first systemic drug approved for this tumor; a phase III trial at diagnosis is ongoing. A
  • Noradrenergic and serotonergic treatments (atomoxetine, α2 agonists, SSRIs) are on the LC and raphe cards. A
  • In Möbius syndrome: surgery for facial palsy (smile surgery) and speech and feeding therapy; the lack of facial expression in social communication can complicate ASD assessment. C

Assessment

Neuropsychological tasks and measures that probe this region's function.

  • Polysomnography (REM sleep)
  • Auditory brainstem responses (ABR)
  • Pupillometry (an indirect index of LC activity — research); cranial nerve examination

Lesions and damage

Clinical findings expected with damage or dysfunction.

  • Locked-in syndrome: ventral pons — consciousness and vertical eye movements are preserved.
  • Internuclear ophthalmoplegia (MLF), Millard-Gubler syndrome.
  • REM sleep behavior disorder.
