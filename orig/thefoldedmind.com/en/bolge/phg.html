Parahippocampal Cortex

Cortex parahippocampalis

Links scene and context information to memory; includes the parahippocampal place area.

Open in the 3D atlas →All regionsParent structure: Inferior Temporal & Fusiform Cortex
AnatomyFunctionsConnections and chemistryDevelopmentLesions and damageIn the modelReferences

Anatomy

  • HCP-MMP areas TF, PHA1–3, PreS, H (Glasser et al., 2016). It sits at the junction of memory regions such as the hippocampus and high-level visual regions such as the fusiform (Aminoff et al., 2013).

Functions

  • Place: responds selectively to scenes; the key factor is information about spatial layout, and empty rooms evoke an equally strong response (Epstein and Kanwisher, 1998). B
  • Context: its roles in visuospatial processing and episodic memory are unified by its place in a network that processes contextual associations (Aminoff et al., 2013). B

Connections and chemistry

  • Hippocampus
  • Entorhinal cortex
  • Retrosplenial cortex
  • Amygdala

Development

Developmental course through childhood and adolescence.

  • The place area grows from ages 7–11 into adulthood, and this growth tracks recognition memory for places (Golarai et al., 2007). B

Lesions and damage

Clinical findings expected with damage or dysfunction.

  • Topographical disorientation is not uniform: its varieties follow damage to different areas, and one is an inability to learn new environments (Aguirre and D'Esposito, 1999). C

In the model

How this structure is defined in the 3D model, and the data source.

HCP-MMP1.0 (Glasser et al., 2016) areas: TF, PHA1, PHA2, PHA3, PreS, H. Each vertex of the region surface in the model was assigned to the area of the nearest HCP voxel; vertices outside this list joined the nearest sub-area along the surface, and the boundary is a smoothed iso-line. This is a group average: boundaries vary between individuals.
