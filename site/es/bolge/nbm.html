Nucleus Basalis of Meynert

Nucleus basalis (Meynert)

The main source of acetylcholine for the neocortex: supports attention, orienting to stimuli and learning; degenerates early in Alzheimer's disease and Lewy body dementias.

Anatomy

  • Scattered clusters of large (magnocellular) neurons in the basal forebrain, below the globus pallidus (substantia innominata) and around the anterior commissure; not a sharply bounded nucleus (Liu et al., 2015).
  • Ch4 in Mesulam's classification: together with the medial septum (Ch1) and the vertical and horizontal limbs of the diagonal band of Broca (Ch2, Ch3) it forms the basal forebrain cholinergic system (Mesulam et al., 1983).
  • Output: the entire neocortex and the amygdala. GABAergic and glutamatergic neurons projecting to the cortex are intermingled with the cholinergic ones (Ballinger et al., 2016).
  • It cannot be distinguished on MRI of the living brain; its location is defined by probabilistic maps derived from post-mortem brains (Zaborszky et al., 2008).

Functions

  • Attention and orienting: cholinergic release rises within seconds to facilitate detection of relevant cues and sustained attention (Hasselmo and Sarter, 2011). B
  • Learning and memory encoding: enhances processing of sensory input in the cortex and hippocampus and permits plasticity.
  • Cortical activation during wakefulness (together with the arousal systems of the brainstem and hypothalamus).

Connections and chemistry

  • Entire neocortex and amygdala (cholinergic output)
  • Medial septum and diagonal band (Ch1–Ch3)
  • Hypothalamus, PPN and LC (arousal inputs)
  • Acetylcholine: muscarinic (M1) and nicotinic (α4β2, α7) receptors

Development

Developmental course through childhood and adolescence.

  • The first cholinesterase activity in the basal forebrain appears at 9 weeks of gestation; at 15 weeks the medial septal, diagonal and basal nuclei segregate and their fibres advance towards the cortex; at 18–22 weeks they reach the transient subplate zone (Kostović, 1986).
  • With ageing the Ch4 region shrinks (Zaborszky et al., 2008; 10 post-mortem brains).

Clinical

  • Alzheimer's disease: more than 75% of neurons in the nucleus basalis are selectively lost; the structural substrate of the cortical cholinergic deficit (Whitehouse et al., 1982). B
  • Loss of basal forebrain volume precedes and predicts degeneration of the entorhinal cortex (Schmitz and Spreng, 2016; ADNI cohort). B
  • Parkinson's disease dementia and dementia with Lewy bodies: Lewy first observed the inclusion bodies named after him in this nucleus; neuronal loss is no less than in Alzheimer's disease (Liu et al., 2015).
  • Drugs that block cholinergic receptors impair attention and memory encoding (Hasselmo and Sarter, 2011); anticholinergic delirium is a common complication of overdose (Dawson and Buckley, 2016). B

Treatment

This region from the perspective of medication, psychotherapy and neuromodulation. Letter badges are evidence levels.

  • Cholinesterase inhibitors stop the enzyme that breaks down acetylcholine; more acetylcholine stays in the synapse and the loss of nucleus basalis neurons is partly offset. Donepezil gives a small but consistent cognitive improvement in Alzheimer's disease (30 trials, 8257 patients; Birks and Harvey, 2018); rivastigmine is also effective in Parkinson's disease dementia (541 patients; Emre et al., 2004). It is a symptomatic treatment (adults). A
  • Deep brain stimulation of the nucleus basalis is experimental: in Alzheimer's disease a 6-patient phase I study found it feasible and well tolerated (Kuhn et al., 2015); in Parkinson's disease dementia a 6-patient double-blind crossover trial showed no improvement in the primary cognitive outcomes (Gratwicke et al., 2018). C

Assessment

Neuropsychological tasks and measures that probe this region's function.

  • Sustained attention and cue detection tasks (CPT)
  • Verbal list learning and memory tests
  • In research: basal forebrain volume on MRI (with a probabilistic map), cholinergic PET

Lesions and damage

Clinical findings expected with damage or dysfunction.

  • Basal forebrain damage, such as rupture of an anterior communicating artery aneurysm, causes a Korsakoff-like amnesia and personality change; the damage involves the septal nuclei, nucleus accumbens and substantia innominata (Damasio et al., 1985; 5 patients). B

In the model

How this structure is defined in the 3D model, and the data source.

Neudorfer et al. (2020) atlas of the hypothalamic region: hand-drawn at 0.25 mm on the average T1 and T2 template of 990 HCP participants (CC BY 4.0). The atlas is in the ICBM 2009b asymmetric template; it was transferred by world coordinates without nonlinear registration (centres within 1.0–1.7 mm of CIT168 for shared structures). The surface was smoothed while preserving volume. The nucleus basalis consists of scattered cell clusters without a sharp boundary; the atlas outline shows the part distinguishable on the template.
