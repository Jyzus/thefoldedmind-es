Arcuate Nucleus

Nucleus arcuatus (infundibularis)

The appetite and hormone centre at the base of the hypothalamus: houses the hunger and satiety neurons, the dopamine neurons that restrain prolactin and the kisspeptin neurons that start puberty.

AnatomyFunctionsClinicalIn the modelReferences

Anatomy

  • At the floor of the third ventricle, just above the median eminence and the pituitary stalk; in this region with a leaky blood–brain barrier it senses hormones in the blood.
  • In humans also called the infundibular nucleus.

Functions

  • Appetite: AgRP/NPY neurons drive hunger and POMC neurons satiety; leptin, insulin and ghrelin act through these two populations (Andermann and Lowell, 2017). B
  • Prolactin: tuberoinfundibular dopamine neurons release dopamine into the portal blood; dopamine restrains prolactin through D2 receptors in the pituitary (Ben-Jonathan and Hnasko, 2001). B
  • Puberty: kisspeptin and its receptor GPR54 start GnRH release; in people with GPR54 mutations puberty does not begin (Seminara et al., 2003). B

Clinical

  • Antipsychotic-induced hyperprolactinaemia: blockade of D2 receptors in the pituitary removes the tuberoinfundibular brake (see Drugs: risperidone).
  • Mutations in the leptin–melanocortin pathway (LEPR, POMC, MC4R) cause severe early-onset obesity (see the Hypothalamus card: setmelanotide).

How this structure is defined in the 3D model, and the data source.

Neudorfer et al. (2020) atlas of the hypothalamic region: hand-drawn at 0.25 mm on the average T1 and T2 template of 990 HCP participants (CC BY 4.0). The atlas is in the ICBM 2009b asymmetric template; it was transferred by world coordinates without nonlinear registration (centres within 1.0–1.7 mm of CIT168 for shared structures).
