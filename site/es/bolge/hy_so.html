Supraoptic Nucleus

Nucleus supraopticus

The large-cell nucleus above the optic tract: carries vasopressin and oxytocin directly to the posterior pituitary and maintains water balance.

Open in the 3D atlas →All regionsParent structure: Hypothalamus & Pituitary
AnatomyFunctionsClinicalIn the modelReferences

Anatomy

  • Dorsolateral to the optic chiasm and optic tract, close to the underside of the brain.
  • Its large (magnocellular) neurons produce vasopressin and oxytocin; their axons pass through the pituitary stalk and end in the posterior pituitary (Swanson and Sawchenko, 1983).

Functions

  • Water balance: when plasma osmolality rises vasopressin is released and the kidney reabsorbs water.
  • Oxytocin release during labour and breastfeeding (together with the large cells of the PVN).

Clinical

  • Central diabetes insipidus: damage to the supraoptic and paraventricular nuclei or the pituitary stalk (craniopharyngioma, Langerhans cell histiocytosis, surgery, trauma) causes polyuria and polydipsia; treated with desmopressin.

In the model

How this structure is defined in the 3D model, and the data source.

Neudorfer et al. (2020) atlas of the hypothalamic region: hand-drawn at 0.25 mm on the average T1 and T2 template of 990 HCP participants (CC BY 4.0). The atlas is in the ICBM 2009b asymmetric template; it was transferred by world coordinates without nonlinear registration (centres within 1.0–1.7 mm of CIT168 for shared structures). The surface was smoothed while preserving volume. The nuclei were clipped to the outer boundary of the hypothalamus (the parent, from CIT168) with a 1 mm margin; this is a representational limit, and the part of the lateral hypothalamus extending beyond it is not shown.
