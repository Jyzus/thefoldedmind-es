Tuberomammillary Nucleus

Nucleus tuberomammillaris

The brain's only source of histamine: supports wakefulness; the target behind the sleepiness and appetite gain caused by many psychiatric drugs.

Open in the 3D atlas →All regionsParent structure: Hypothalamus & Pituitary
AnatomyFunctionsClinicalTreatmentIn the modelReferences

Anatomy

  • Scattered large neurons at the posterior base of the hypothalamus, in front of and beside the mammillary body.
  • Histamine fibres spread to almost the whole brain, above all the cortex, thalamus and other arousal regions (Scammell et al., 2019).

Functions

  • Wakefulness: histamine neurons fire during waking and fall silent in sleep (Haas and Panula, 2003); reducing their activity produces sedation (Scammell et al., 2019). B
  • Receives excitatory input from orexin neurons; during sleep it is inhibited by the ventrolateral preoptic nucleus (Saper et al., 2005).

Clinical

  • Drugs that block the H1 receptor cause sleepiness and increased appetite: across 17 antipsychotics, H1 receptor binding affinity predicted short-term weight gain, and olanzapine and quetiapine are among the strongest H1 binders (Kroeze et al., 2003). Sleepiness, increased appetite and weight gain are also the commonest side effects of mirtazapine (Anttila and Leinonen, 2001). First-generation antihistamines belong to this group too (see Drugs). B

Treatment

This region from the perspective of medication, psychotherapy and neuromodulation. Letter badges are evidence levels.

  • Pitolisant (H3 receptor inverse agonist) increases histamine release and reduces daytime sleepiness and cataplexy in narcolepsy (Scammell et al., 2019); FDA-approved for daytime sleepiness from age 6 (2024). A

In the model

How this structure is defined in the 3D model, and the data source.

Neudorfer et al. (2020) atlas of the hypothalamic region: hand-drawn at 0.25 mm on the average T1 and T2 template of 990 HCP participants (CC BY 4.0). The atlas is in the ICBM 2009b asymmetric template; it was transferred by world coordinates without nonlinear registration (centres within 1.0–1.7 mm of CIT168 for shared structures). The surface was smoothed while preserving volume. The nuclei were clipped to the outer boundary of the hypothalamus (the parent, from CIT168) with a 1 mm margin; this is a representational limit, and the part of the lateral hypothalamus extending beyond it is not shown.
