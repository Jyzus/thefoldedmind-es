Mesencephalon (Midbrain)

Mesencephalon

The source of dopamine: movement (substantia nigra) and reward–motivation (VTA); defense, pain and eye reflexes.

Anatomy

  • Between the pons and the diencephalon; the cerebral aqueduct (of Sylvius) runs through its center.
  • Tectum: superior colliculus (visual orienting, saccades), inferior colliculus (auditory).
  • Tegmentum: substantia nigra pars compacta (nigrostriatal dopamine), ventral tegmental area (VTA — mesolimbic/mesocortical dopamine), red nucleus, periaqueductal gray (PAG), nuclei of cranial nerves III and IV.
  • Cerebral peduncles (crus cerebri): corticospinal and corticopontine fibers. Blood supply: PCA, superior cerebellar artery.

Functions

  • Dopaminergic systems: movement (SNc) and reward–motivation–learning (VTA → NAcc, PFC).
  • Reflexive gaze and head orienting (superior colliculus), auditory orienting.
  • PAG: defensive behaviors (freezing/flight), endogenous opioid pain control, vocalization.
  • Eye movements (III, IV), pupillary reflex (Edinger-Westphal), wakefulness (reticular formation).

Connections and chemistry

  • Nigrostriatal pathway (SNc → dorsal striatum)
  • Mesolimbic pathway (VTA → NAcc, amygdala, hippocampus)
  • Mesocortical pathway (VTA → PFC)
  • PAG ↔ amygdala, hypothalamus
  • Superior colliculus ↔ FEF, pulvinar

Development

Developmental course through childhood and adolescence.

  • Mesocortical dopamine projections are reorganized in adolescence; striatal dopamine receptors peak in adolescence (animal studies) — reward sensitivity.
  • The newborn’s orienting to faces is partly explained by a subcortical (superior colliculus–pulvinar) route (Johnson).

Clinical

  • ADHD: the dopamine hypothesis; methylphenidate and amphetamines act on the dopamine transporter (DAT).
  • Schizophrenia: mesolimbic hyperdopaminergia (positive symptoms), mesocortical hypodopaminergia (negative/cognitive symptoms).
  • Substance use: plasticity in VTA dopamine neurons.
  • Tourette syndrome: dopamine hypersensitivity; D2 antagonist and partial agonist treatments.
  • Pineal region tumors (adolescents): compression of the dorsal midbrain — Parinaud syndrome, aqueductal stenosis and hydrocephalus.

Treatment

This region from the perspective of medication, psychotherapy and neuromodulation. Letter badges are evidence levels.

  • Treatments targeting the dopamine system (stimulants, antipsychotics, the new non-D2 antipsychotic xanomeline-trospium) are on the VTA and SN cards. A
  • Hydrocephalus from aqueductal stenosis in pineal region tumors: endoscopic third ventriculostomy or a shunt; tumor-specific surgery, chemotherapy, radiotherapy. A
  • Diffuse midline glioma (H3 K27M) can also arise in the midbrain and thalamus; treatment is on the pons card.

Assessment

Neuropsychological tasks and measures that probe this region's function.

  • Pupillary light and near reflexes, eye movement examination
  • PET dopamine imaging (research)
  • Reward prediction error paradigms

Lesions and damage

Clinical findings expected with damage or dysfunction.

  • Parinaud syndrome: upgaze palsy, light–near dissociation.
  • Weber syndrome: ipsilateral third nerve palsy + contralateral hemiparesis.
  • Peduncular hallucinosis; aqueductal stenosis → obstructive hydrocephalus.
