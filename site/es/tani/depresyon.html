Psychopathology · diagnosis

Depression

A mood disorder in which low mood (in children and adolescents often irritable mood) lasting at least two weeks, loss of interest and pleasure (anhedonia), and changes in sleep, appetite, energy, attention and feelings of worthlessness impair functioning. Reward, stress response and emotion regulation circuits stand out in the brain.

Clinical picture (children and adolescents)

  • In children and adolescents, irritability, bodily complaints, falling school performance and social withdrawal may be more prominent than low mood. A
  • Prevalence: point prevalence of major depression in adolescents is about 8% and lifetime prevalence 19%; elevated self-reported depressive symptoms 34% (not a diagnosis). More common in girls (Shorey et al., 2022). A
  • After puberty, rates rise markedly in girls; depression beginning in adolescence carries a risk of recurrence. A
  • Co-occurring conditions: anxiety disorders, ADHD, substance use; suicidal thoughts and behavior must always be asked about. A

Circuits and networks

  • Anterior Cingulate Cortex (especially the subgenual part): negative emotion and self-evaluation; a stimulation target in treatment-resistant depression. B
  • Orbitofrontal Cortex and Medial & Frontopolar Prefrontal Cortex: reward appraisal and self-referential thinking; in ENIGMA, adults had thinner orbitofrontal, cingulate, insular and temporal cortex (Schmaal et al., 2017). A
  • Hippocampus: linked to stress and cortisol; smaller volume, especially in recurrent depression and depression beginning before age 21 (Schmaal et al., 2016). A
  • Amygdala: increased response to negative stimuli. B
  • Nucleus Accumbens and Ventral Tegmental Area: reward anticipation and motivation; the response to reward is reduced in anhedonia. B
  • Habenula (habenula): the "expected reward did not arrive" signal; it suppresses the dopamine and serotonin nuclei and is linked to depression in animal models. B
  • Hypothalamus & Pituitary: the hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal (HPA) axis; the stress response. A
  • Dorsolateral Prefrontal Cortex: cognitive control; the target of repetitive transcranial magnetic stimulation (rTMS). A
  • Networks: increased connectivity within the Default mode network (DMN) and rumination (going over negative thoughts), reduced control by the Frontoparietal control network. B

Neurotransmitter systems

  • Serotonin (serotonin): SSRIs are effective; but the simple "serotonin deficiency" explanation is inadequate, and the effect emerges over weeks through neuroplasticity. B
  • Dopamine (dopamine): reward and motivation; linked to anhedonia. B
  • Noradrenaline (noradrenaline): energy and arousal; the target of SNRIs and some antidepressants. B
  • Glutamate: the rapid antidepressant effect of ketamine works through this system; esketamine is approved in adults, evidence in adolescents is limited. B

Imaging and neurobiological findings

  • ENIGMA-MDD subcortical: smaller hippocampal volume; the difference is clear in recurrent depression and early onset (≤ 21 years) (Schmaal et al., 2016). A
  • ENIGMA-MDD cortex (2,148 patients, 7,957 controls): in adults, thinner orbitofrontal, anterior and posterior cingulate, insular and temporal cortex; in adolescents no thickness difference but smaller total surface area (frontal and sensorimotor regions), most in recurrent cases (Schmaal et al., 2017). A
  • These are group-level differences; imaging cannot diagnose an individual. A

Developmental course

  • Brain findings in adolescent-onset depression differ from adult-onset depression (surface area rather than thickness), suggesting a different effect on the developing brain (Schmaal et al., 2017). B
  • Early-life stress and adverse childhood experiences raise the risk of depression through the HPA axis and the hippocampus. B

Treatment and the systems it targets

  • In adolescents (ages 12–17, 439 patients), response at 12 weeks: fluoxetine + CBT 71.0%, fluoxetine alone 60.6%, CBT alone 43.2%, placebo 34.8%; combined treatment had the best benefit-risk balance (TADS, 2004). By week 36, CBT alone and fluoxetine alone had also approached combined treatment. A
  • In mild depression, psychotherapy (CBT, interpersonal therapy) and support first; in moderate and severe depression, medication in addition to psychotherapy. A
  • Medication: FDA-approved drugs for children and adolescents are fluoxetine (age 8 and up) and escitalopram (age 12 and up). Antidepressants may increase the risk of suicidal thoughts under age 25 (boxed warning); close monitoring is needed. A
  • In resistant depression, rTMS (left DLPFC) and electroconvulsive therapy are options; in adolescents they are used in specialist centers. B
  • Electroconvulsive therapy (ECT): In a meta-analysis of adult studies it is superior to antidepressant drugs. In adolescents the evidence is a practice parameter based on case reports and small studies; symptom and cognitive assessment are recommended before and after, and preadolescent children are outside the parameter. In an MRI study of 281 patients, hippocampal volume rises about 0.28% per session, but this does not explain improvement. A
  • Ketamine and esketamine: In adult resistant depression, intranasal esketamine added to an oral antidepressant showed a rapid, dose-related effect (67 patients); the 2019 FDA approval is for adults and it is given at certified sites. In a crossover trial of 17 adolescents, a single intravenous dose of ketamine beat midazolam at 24 hours, with more blood pressure rise and dissociative symptoms; long-term safety data are lacking. It is thought to act through the glutamate system. B
  • Interpersonal psychotherapy: In a school-based clinic trial of 63 adolescents, 12 sessions of interpersonal psychotherapy reduced symptoms more and improved functioning more than usual care. B
  • Other antidepressants: In a network meta-analysis of 21 antidepressants across 522 trials in adults, all beat placebo. The picture differs in children and adolescents: in a network meta-analysis, fluoxetine was the only drug significantly better than placebo. The suicidal-thoughts warning applies under age 25. A
