Clomipramine

Tricyclic antidepressant

A tricyclic antidepressant that strongly inhibits serotonin reuptake; noradrenaline reuptake is inhibited by the drug and its active metabolite desmethylclomipramine. In Turkey it is indicated in children and adolescents only for OCD (ages 10–17); its anticholinergic, cardiac and seizure side effects call for monitoring.

Site of action

Target: SERT and NET blockade; according to the Turkish product information (KÜB), inhibition of serotonin reuptake is the more important. On PET, even a low dose occupied about 80% of thalamic SERT, and occupancy at clinical doses was also about 80% (Suhara et al., 2003). B

With the serotonin transporter (SERT) blocked, serotonin stays longer in the synapse: serotonin rises at the targets along the pathways leaving the raphe. SERT is densest in the raphe, thalamus, hypothalamus, striatum and amygdala (Beliveau et al., 2017). Noradrenaline reuptake is also inhibited: in monkeys, clomipramine and desmethylclomipramine occupied the noradrenaline transporter (NET) (Takano et al., 2011); in humans, NET is densest in the medial thalamus and around the locus coeruleus (Takano et al., 2008), so the noradrenaline pathway is drawn only from the locus coeruleus to the thalamus. The evidence for the noradrenaline part is weaker (level C: occupancy has not been measured in humans). B

The anatomy of side effects

Anticholinergic effects

No brain pathway drawn: no muscarinic occupancy measured in the brain

Dry mouth, constipation, blurred vision and micturition disorders are very common; it is not used in narrow-angle glaucoma or urinary retention (KÜB). A

Sleepiness, appetite and weight

No brain pathway drawn: no H1 occupancy measured in the brain

Sleepiness, increased appetite and weight gain are very common; the KÜB describes its antihistaminic effect. A

Heart rhythm and blood pressure

No brain pathway drawn

Fast heart rate, palpitations and a drop in blood pressure on standing are common; conduction disorders and QT prolongation are very rare, with torsades de pointes in hypokalemia. Not used after a recent heart attack, in heart block or in arrhythmias (KÜB). A

Seizure threshold

No brain pathway drawn

It lowers the seizure threshold, and the risk is dose-related; it is used with great caution in epilepsy and with predisposing factors (KÜB). A

Overdose

No brain pathway drawn

In overdose, cardiac and nervous system complications are the main danger; in children, accidental ingestion of any amount is serious and possibly fatal (KÜB). A

Children and adolescents

  • Turkey (product information, KÜB, checked in November 2026): indicated for obsessive-compulsive syndromes at ages 10–17; there is no experience under age 10. Not recommended in children and adolescents for depressive states, phobias, panic attacks, cataplexy or chronic pain because evidence is insufficient.
  • FDA: OCD from age 10.
  • In an 8-week multicenter double-blind trial in child and adolescent OCD, CY-BOCS scores fell by 37% with clomipramine and by 8% with placebo (DeVeaugh-Geiss et al., 1992). A
  • In a meta-analysis of 12 medication trials in pediatric OCD (1,044 participants), the drug effect was moderate (SMD 0.46); with other factors controlled, clomipramine was superior to each SSRI (Geller et al., 2003). A
  • American Academy of Child and Adolescent Psychiatry guideline (AACAP, 2012): SSRIs are the first-line medications for pediatric OCD; before clomipramine, a child has a cardiac evaluation and ECG; with a partial response, adding clomipramine to an SSRI may help. B
  • Antidepressant warning: in young people up to age 24, suicidal thinking and behavior may increase; close monitoring at the start and with dose changes (KÜB). A

Interactions

  • Not given together with MAO inhibitors or within 3 weeks before or after them; also contraindicated with reversible MAO-A inhibitors such as moclobemide (KÜB). A
  • Serotonin syndrome can occur with SSRIs, SNRIs, other tricyclics and lithium; a 2–3 week washout is advised before or after fluoxetine (KÜB). A
  • Broken down by CYP2D6: fluoxetine, paroxetine and sertraline raise its level; fluvoxamine raises clomipramine levels about 4-fold and desmethylclomipramine levels 2-fold (KÜB). A
  • Should not be combined with drugs that prolong the QT interval (some antiarrhythmics, phenothiazines, pimozide, lithium); hypokalemia caused by diuretics raises the risk (KÜB). A
  • Methylphenidate can raise tricyclic levels; with antipsychotics levels can rise and the seizure threshold can fall; it reduces the blood-pressure-lowering effect of clonidine (KÜB). A
