Library · medication · Antidepressants (SSRIs)

Fluoxetine

SSRI

The only antidepressant significantly better than placebo in a network meta-analysis of child and adolescent depression (2016); its very long half-life and strong CYP2D6 inhibition call for care with interactions.

Site of action

Target: SERT blockade; at the lowest effective dose 76–85% of SERT is occupied (Meyer et al., 2004). B

With the serotonin transporter (SERT) blocked, serotonin stays longer in the synapse: serotonin rises at the targets along the pathways leaving the raphe. SERT is densest in the raphe, thalamus, hypothalamus, striatum and amygdala (Beliveau et al., 2017). Emotional processing changes in the first week: the amygdala’s threat response falls before mood improves (Godlewska et al., 2012); the clinical response comes over weeks. B

Serotonin Pathways

Children and adolescents

  • FDA: depression ages 8–18, OCD ages 7–17. EMA: from age 8, for moderate to severe depression that has not responded to psychotherapy, together with psychotherapy.
  • Turkey: not recommended for children and adolescents under 18 in the Turkish product information (KÜB) of the generic products checked in October 2026 (the most recent from 2021); may differ by product.
  • The only drug significantly better than placebo for child and adolescent depression in a network meta-analysis (Cipriani et al., 2016); in a later Cochrane network meta-analysis the benefit was small (Hetrick et al., 2021). A
  • TADS: in adolescent depression, response at week 12 was 71% with fluoxetine + CBT and 35% with placebo. A
  • Antidepressant boxed warning: suicidal thinking under age 25; close monitoring at the start. A

Interactions

  • Strong CYP2D6 inhibitor: raises the levels of atomoxetine, aripiprazole and risperidone; their labels call for dose adjustment. A
  • Inhibition lasts 3 weeks or longer after stopping; wait at least 5 weeks before switching to an MAOI. A
  • Serotonin syndrome: together with other serotonergic drugs.
