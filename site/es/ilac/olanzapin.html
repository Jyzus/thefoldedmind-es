Olanzapine

Second-generation antipsychotic

Found effective in adolescent schizophrenia and bipolar mania; among the highest weight and metabolic burdens in pediatric comparisons, and the FDA label says other drugs may be considered first in adolescents. Broad receptor profile (5-HT2A, D2, H1, muscarinic, 5-HT2C).

Site of action

Target: 5-HT2A and D2 antagonist; strong H1 and muscarinic blockade; D2 occupancy 43–89% with continued use (Kapur et al., 1999). B

Blocks D2 (striatum) and 5-HT2A (cortex) receptors; because it also strongly blocks histamine H1 and muscarinic receptors, sleepiness, appetite and anticholinergic effects are prominent. B

The anatomy of side effects

Sleepiness, appetite and weight

Histamine (H1) and 5-HT2C: hypothalamus

The highest weight gain in studies comparing second-generation antipsychotics in children and adolescents: +8.5 kg in ~11 weeks, with rises in cholesterol, triglycerides and insulin (Correll et al., 2009; Sikich et al., 2008). In an analysis comparing antipsychotics, H1 affinity was the receptor affinity most strongly linked to weight gain (Kroeze et al., 2003). B

Anticholinergic effects

Muscarinic receptors: cortex and thalamus

SPECT has shown that it blocks muscarinic receptors in the brain: occupancy in the cortex, thalamus and pons (7 patients; Raedler et al., 2000). The cognitive load adds up with other anticholinergic drugs (see the acetylcholine card). Dry mouth and constipation come from receptors in the body. B

Movement side effects

Nigrostriatal pathway: SNc → putamen and caudate

Extrapyramidal effects rise with dose; the likelihood becomes clear once D2 occupancy exceeds 78% (PET study with haloperidol; Kapur et al., 2000). B

Children and adolescents

  • FDA: schizophrenia and bipolar I ages 13–17; the label says other drugs may be considered first in adolescents because of weight and lipid gains.
  • Olanzapine + fluoxetine combination: bipolar I depression ages 10–17 (FDA).
  • EMA: not recommended under 18. Turkey (product information, KÜB, checked in October 2026): indicated for schizophrenia ages 13–17, with a weight and lipid warning.
  • In early-onset schizophrenia (TEOSS) not superior to molindone, with the heaviest metabolic burden (Sikich et al., 2008). B

Interactions

  • Broken down by CYP1A2: smoking lowers its levels and fluvoxamine raises them; carbamazepine increases its clearance by about 50% (FDA label). A
  • Effects add up with other sedative and anticholinergic drugs.
