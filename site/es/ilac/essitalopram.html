Escitalopram

SSRI

The active enantiomer of citalopram; approved in the US for adolescent depression and for generalized anxiety in children and adolescents; not recommended under 18 in the EU product information and the Turkish product information (KÜB) checked (October 2026).

Site of action

Target: SERT blockade; a single dose occupies 59–69% of SERT (Lundberg et al., 2007); with continued use 76–85%, as for the class. B

With the serotonin transporter (SERT) blocked, serotonin stays longer in the synapse: serotonin rises at the targets along the pathways leaving the raphe. SERT is densest in the raphe, thalamus, hypothalamus, striatum and amygdala (Beliveau et al., 2017). Emotional processing changes in the first week: the amygdala’s threat response falls before mood improves (Godlewska et al., 2012); the clinical response comes over weeks. B

Children and adolescents

  • FDA: depression ages 12–17 (2009), generalized anxiety age 7 and over (2023).
  • EU product information and four Turkish product information documents (KÜB) checked in October 2026: should not be used under 18.
  • Adolescent generalized anxiety: PARS reduction −8.65 vs −3.52; blood levels fell as CYP2C19 metabolism got faster (Strawn et al., 2020). B

Interactions

  • May moderately inhibit CYP2D6: desipramine exposure doubled (FDA label). A
  • CYP2C19 genotype changes blood levels; CPIC recommends genotype-guided dosing. A
  • Dose-dependent QT prolongation: caution with other QT-prolonging drugs (e.g. quetiapine) (Castro et al., 2013). B
